Research experience

Jovian polar vortex formation

December 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

I developed a polar γ-plane shallow-water solver with vorticity-dependent, quasi-stochastic convection and used vortex tracking and Biot–Savart analysis to study collective drift. Stronger convective feedback promoted vortex growth and the formation of vorticity shields. Nearby and remote flows contributed to drift in opposite directions.

Convective organization over complex topography

October 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

Through the College Students’ Innovative Training Plan, I investigate how terrain blocking and friction shape mesoscale convection in coastal South China. I build on a shallow-water prototype to design idealized experiments with parameterized moist convection and topographic effects.

Rotating-fluid laboratory experiments

June 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

At NJU’s Student Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Lab, I helped develop a rotating water tank and conducted experiments on Taylor columns, rotating boiling flows, the beta effect, and vortex interactions.

Internal variability in East Asian summer monsoon projections

June – August 2025

Advised by Sandro M. Ferreira Veiga

In the Sino-Finnish Research Training Program, I used empirical orthogonal function analysis of North Pacific sea surface temperature anomalies to identify phases of the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO), then compared rainfall composites from CMIP6 MIROC6 simulations. Projected rainfall varied across three emissions scenarios, and opposite PDO phases produced broadly opposing rainfall anomalies over eastern China.
