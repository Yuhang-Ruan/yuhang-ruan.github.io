Research experience

Jovian polar vortex formation

December 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

I developed a polar γ-plane shallow-water solver with vorticity-dependent, quasi-stochastic convection to test how convective feedback affects the formation of Jupiter’s polar vortices. The simulations produced shielded cyclones and persistent vortex crystals. A conventional Rossby-wave phase-speed estimate could not explain the collective drift. Biot–Savart diagnostics showed that other vortices and the background vorticity contributed to the drift in opposite directions.

arXiv:2611.02372

Convective organization over complex topography

October 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

I study convective initiation and organization over complex terrain in coastal South China. Drawing on my collaborator Yujie Huang’s radar tracking, I developed a shallow-water solver with parameterized convection to investigate how terrain blocking and friction affect the observed convective patterns.

Rotating-fluid laboratory experiments

June 2025 – present

Advised by Hao Fu

At NJU’s Student Geophysical Fluid Dynamics Lab, I helped develop a rotating water tank and conducted experiments on Taylor columns, rotating boiling flows, the beta effect, and vortex interactions.

Internal variability in East Asian summer monsoon projections

June – August 2025

Advised by Sandro M. Ferreira Veiga

In the Sino-Finnish Research Training Program, I used empirical orthogonal function analysis of North Pacific sea surface temperature anomalies to identify phases of the Pacific Decadal Oscillation (PDO), then compared rainfall composites from CMIP6 MIROC6 simulations. Projected rainfall varied across three emissions scenarios, and opposite PDO phases produced broadly opposing rainfall anomalies over eastern China.
